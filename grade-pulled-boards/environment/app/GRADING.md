# Works standard - grading sawn oak

This is the whole of the mill's grading. Every figure the grader works to is
here.

A book is kept at each bench and the benches do not all keep it alike. The
grader works from the book as it stands, and where an entry cannot be right
against this standard he puts it right before he grades the board.

## What a grade asks for

A board is graded on the **yield** of its face: the part of the face that can
be got out in clear cuttings, as a percentage of the face.

| grade | yield needed |
| --- | --- |
| Firsts | 83 per cent |
| Seconds | 75 |
| Selects | 67 |
| No.1 Common | 60 |
| No.2 Common | 50 |
| Sound Wormy | anything less |

A board has two faces and **takes the grade of its poorer face**.

## The cuttings

A cutting is a clear rectangle **75 mm wide** and **at least 600 mm long**.

The face is laid out in strips 75 mm wide running the length of the board. The
lay-out **begins at the near edge of the sound width** and runs across towards
the far edge: that is, at the near edge of the board where there is no wane on
that side, and at the inner edge of the wane where there is. Any part of the
width left over at the far end, less than a strip wide, yields nothing.

In each strip the clear runs between the defects are taken. A run counts only
if it is at least the least cutting long, and a run longer than that **counts
at its whole length** - it is not broken up into cuttings of the least size. A
defect blocks the run of every strip it lies across, over the length it covers:
a knot over its greatest dimension along the board, a shake over its length.

The yield is the total area of the cuttings as a percentage of the face, and
the face is the board's length by the sound width. Work it to the nearest whole
per cent, halves up.

## Wane

Wane is the bark edge left along a side of a board. The **sound width** is the
board's width less the wane at its worst point. Wane is booked by the board, by
the edge it is on - `near` or `far` - and by the width it takes.

Which edge it is on matters as much as how much it takes, because the lay-out
begins at the near edge of the sound width: wane on the near edge therefore
moves the whole lay-out across the board, while wane on the far edge takes the
same width off the far end and does not move the lay-out at all.

## Measuring

Sizes are in millimetres. A defect is booked by where its centre lies along the
board and across it, and by its size.

**A knot is measured by its greatest dimension.** A knot in oak grows longer
along the grain than it is across it.

A knot is a branch. A branch that ran right through the log shows on both faces
of the board and is booked on each of them; one that died in the tree, or that
the saw missed, shows on one face only. A board is graded face by face, on the
defects booked for that face and no others.

## Moisture

This standard is written for boards brought down to **fifteen per cent**. Oak
loses **four parts in a hundred across the grain** in coming down to it, and
nothing to speak of along the grain. A board booked green is therefore wider
than it will be, and everything measured across it - the wane, and where the
defects sit across the face - comes in by the same four parts in a hundred.
Lengths and the run of a defect along the board do not move.

Whether a board was measured green or dry is booked against it.
