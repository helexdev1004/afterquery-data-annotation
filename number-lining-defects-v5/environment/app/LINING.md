# The tunnels' lining and how it was recorded

This note is the whole of what the asset team holds on how the five bored
tunnels were lined and how last year's surveys were booked.

The five inspectors did not all book alike. Where an entry cannot be right
against this note it is put right before it is used.

## Driving and lining

A tunnel's two portals are called A and B, A being the portal its chainage is
reckoned from. Each tunnel was bored from both, drive A from portal A and drive
B from portal B, and the two drives met underground. The lining is precast
concrete rings, built one after another behind each machine.

## Ring numbers and segments

Each drive numbered its rings as it built them, from 1 at its own portal, and
the number was painted on the ring: `T3-A-0212` is the 212th ring that drive A
built in tunnel 3. `tunnels.csv` gives the number of rings each drive built in
each tunnel.

A ring is six segments, lettered A to F clockwise looking up-chainage. A holds
twelve and one o'clock, B two and three, C four and five, D six and seven, E
eight and nine, and F ten and eleven. The asset register files a defect under
its ring and its segment, written `T3-A-0212-C`.

## The joint survey

The survey team taped each tunnel through and booked where every joint between
rings falls along the tape, in metres to the millimetre. Joint 0 is the portal
face, and ring n of the survey lies between joint n-1 and joint n.

## The walk-through inspection

The inspector walked the survey team's tape and booked every defect he found:
what it was, where it falls along the tape in metres, to the middle of the
defect, its clock position round the ring as he faced along the tape, and its
extent in metres.

A defect whose middle falls exactly on a joint is filed under the ring beyond
the joint, the one further along the tape.

## Measures

The team works in metres. A foot is 0.3048 m and a yard is 0.9144 m.
