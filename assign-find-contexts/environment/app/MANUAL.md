# Site recording manual

This is the whole of how the site was recorded. Every figure and every rule the
archive was kept to is here.

Each trench had its own supervisor, and the supervisors did not all keep the
archive alike. Where an entry cannot be right against this manual it is put
right before it is used.

## Datum and depths

Site datum stands at **100.00 m above Ordnance datum**. Every depth in the
archive is a **depth below site datum**, in metres, positive downwards. The
ground itself lies below datum everywhere on the site.

Depths and offsets are booked to the nearest centimetre.

## The trenches

Every trench is a long rectangle running east and west, taped from a **zero
peg** at one end, and its length is recorded.

Every trench also has its own **temporary bench mark**, a peg beside the trench
whose depth below site datum is recorded. Levels in a trench were taken from
its bench mark and **reduced to site datum before they were booked**.

## The two teams

Every trench was dug by two teams, one working out from the zero peg and the
other working in from the far end. The point where they met is taped and
recorded with the trench, as `meeting_m`.

## The section drawings

Each trench has two long sections, one along its north side and one along its
south side. Each was drawn in the field, and the drawings were digitised as
drawn: each boundary is a run of points, every point an offset along the
drawing and a depth below site datum, and the boundary runs **straight from
each point to the next**.

A context on a drawing lies between its top boundary and its bottom boundary.
The layers lie one on another from the ground down. Where a layer thins to
nothing its top and bottom boundaries meet. The layers run right across the
trenches, so a trench's two sections show the same layers, though seldom at the
same thickness on both sides of the trench.

## Finds

A small find is recorded where it lay. It is plotted onto the drawing of
whichever of the trench's two long sections it lay nearer, and booked with
the offset it has on that drawing and its depth below site datum. It is that
drawing which says what deposit it lay in.

## Cut features

A pit is dug down from the surface of its day through whatever lay beneath, and
later fills up. On a drawing a cut feature's outline therefore crosses the
layers it was dug through. **A cut feature removed what stood where it was dug:
a find inside a cut feature's outline belongs to that feature's fill, and not to
any layer the outline crosses.** No pit on this site was wide enough to reach
both of a trench's sections, so each is drawn in one of them only. Cut features
were numbered on each drawing as the draughtsman came to them, the north
drawing's from 51 and the south drawing's from 61.

## Context numbers

Contexts are numbered by trench, trench 3's from 3001. A trench's layers are
numbered from the ground down. Those are the numbers of the team that worked
out from the zero peg, and the drawings carry them from end to end. The team
that worked in from the far end numbered the same deposits **a hundred
higher**: what the drawings call 3004 it dug as 3104, and a pit the drawings
call 3052 it dug as 3152. **A find takes the context number of the team that
dug it**, which is the team on whose side of the meeting point it lay.

## A find on a boundary

A find lying exactly on a boundary is taken with the context **below** the
boundary.
