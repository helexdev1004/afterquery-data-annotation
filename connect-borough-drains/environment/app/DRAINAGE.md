# Borough Surveyor's standing instructions - drainage

These are the rules the estate was surveyed to. They are complete: every figure
the office works to is here.

## Levels

Levels are booked in millimetres above Ordnance datum. A chamber is booked
twice, once at the cover and once at the invert, and the invert is the floor of
the channel.

The borough kept its own datum until the survey of 1931, and some level books
have never been brought forward. **The old borough datum stands 2.740 m below
Ordnance datum.** A level worked to the old datum therefore reads 2740 mm
higher than the same point worked to Ordnance. Each book is lettered in the
chamber book so the office can tell which surveyor's work it is; the letter
says whose book it was and nothing about which datum he used.

A sewer falls to its outfall. Every chamber stands above the chamber it falls
into.

## Drains

A private drain runs in a straight line from the property to the chamber it
discharges into, and is laid to a fall of **not less than 1 in 100 and not more
than 1 in 30**. The fall is reckoned on the plan distance between the property
and the chamber, taken to the nearest metre.

A property's discharge is booked to the nearest five litres an hour.

## The flow survey

A gauge is set in every chamber, in the **outgoing** channel, and reads
everything standing above it - every drain on that chamber and every drain on
every chamber that falls into it. It follows that a chamber can never gauge
less than the chambers that fall into it.

The rate is a settled dry-weather flow, so a gauge reads alike whenever it is
visited and one reading is taken for the survey.

## The tracer survey

Dye is put into a drain at the property and watched for at the chambers an
observer can be posted at. **It is seen at every chamber below the drain and at
no other**, so a sighting says the drain stands somewhere above that chamber
and does not say which chamber it discharges into.

The dye travels at **a quarter of a metre a second** along the pipe, measured
from the property, down the drain, and along every length of sewer to the
observer. Times are booked in whole minutes from the start of the survey, both
when the dye was put in and when it was seen.

## Rain

The estate is on a combined system and **it surcharges in heavy rain**. A sewer
running surcharged is full and under head, and what is in it does not keep to
the fall of the pipe. The borough's rain gauge is booked in twenty minute
periods through the survey; five millimetres in a period is heavy rain.
